\section{What these mocks can and cannot validate}
\label{sec:limits}

A validation against mocks tests the model and the mocks together. Three properties of the mocks limit what can be concluded.

\paragraph{Long radial modes.} In a thin redshift shell ($0.4<z<0.6$ is $\simeq450\,\Mpch$ deep) super-sample covariance is dominated by radial modes of wavelength comparable to the shell depth, which change the mean density of the shell. We measured how much of them the mocks contain from the weighted $n(z)$ of all $\val{nNZmocks}$ holi mocks (\cref{fig:nz}). After the Poisson part is removed, the rms of the counts in slabs of thickness $L$ is compared with linear theory, $(b_1+f)\,\sigma_{\rm slab}(L)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item For the clean LRG1 mocks the ratio is $\val{nz/LRG1/NGC/clean/23}$--$\val{nz/LRG1/NGC/clean/91}$ for $L\lesssim100\,\Mpch$ and falls to $\val{nz/LRG1/NGC/clean/454}$ (NGC) and $\val{nz/LRG1/SGC/clean/454}$ (SGC) for the whole shell. The mocks therefore contain about 60\% of the linear-theory variance of the shell-scale mode. The twelve defective mocks alone would raise the whole-shell ratio to $\val{nz/LRG1/NGC/all/454}$ (NGC).
\item For QSO, a much deeper sample, the ratio is $\simeq0.9$--$0.95$ up to $\sim900\,\Mpch$. The whole-shell value ($\val{nz/QSO/NGC/clean/1740}$, NGC) is dominated by the Poisson correction, which ignores the weights, and is not reliable.
\end{itemize}
An earlier estimate from eight mocks suggested a much larger deficit; it was dominated by noise. The realised normalisation of the estimator scatters by only $0.43$\% (NGC) and $0.75$\% (SGC), against $0.99$\% and $1.39$\% predicted from the window's long-mode variance. That is about half of what the measured shell counts imply, so the normalisation responds only partly to the shell-mean density (the randoms of each mock follow its own $n(z)$) \todo{settle; consequence for the local-average term}. For the data, which contain all long modes, the model's SSC is the more faithful description; the mocks underestimate the isotropic shell-scale term by $\simeq40$\% in variance.

\paragraph{Correlated caps.} The AbacusSummit caps are cut from the same boxes, and their power spectra are correlated at $\simeq0.15$ on large scales. The holi caps share long modes as well: their total LRG and QSO counts are correlated at $\val{nzcapcorr/LRG1}$ and $\val{nzcapcorr/QSO}$ over the mocks, although their power-spectrum bins are not. A combined-cap covariance that assumes independent caps underestimates the scatter of the shell-mean mode in the mocks, while the data caps are independent. The holi caps are uncorrelated (mean correlation of the $P_0$ bins at $k<0.06\,\hMpc$ below $0.01$ over the $\val{Nmocks}$ mocks).

\paragraph{Statistical power.} With $N=\val{Nmocks}$, a single variance ratio is known to $\val{wishart}$\%, the whitened trace over $n=\val{nbins}$ elements to $\simeq0.4$\%, and a template amplitude to the Fisher errors of \cref{fig:templates}. Terms that change the covariance by less than $\simeq1$\% in every direction cannot be validated with these mocks. The 25 AbacusSummit mocks constrain only the largest terms.
