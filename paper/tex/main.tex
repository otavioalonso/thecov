\title{Validating analytic covariance matrices of DESI DR2 power-spectrum multipoles with mocks: what each term does, and how to tell}

\author[a]{\todo{author list}}
\affiliation[a]{\todo{affiliations}}

\abstract{We validate an analytic covariance model for the power-spectrum multipoles of DESI DR2 luminous red galaxies (LRG, $0.4<z<0.6$) and quasars against $\sim850$ approximate mocks and 25 $N$-body (AbacusSummit) mocks. The model, implemented in \thecov, combines a windowed Gaussian term, super-sample covariance with the local-average effect, and the two discreteness four-point terms, all computed from the survey randoms with nothing fitted to the mocks. We find that (i) the local approximation to the survey window, $W=m^2$, underestimates the LRG Gaussian variance by $\simeq\val{kernelgain/LRG1/NGC}$\% because of small-scale structure in the veto mask, which a pair-averaged window built from the correlation function itself recovers; (ii) the remaining LRG excess is dominated by two coherent amplitude modes of the monopole and quadrupole, the signature of super-sample covariance, which the model captures, reducing $\langle\chi^2\rangle/n$ at $k<0.3\,\hMpc$ from $\val{trace/LRG1/NGC/G/0.3}$ to $\val{trace/LRG1/NGC/rec/0.3}$; (iii) the tree-level and response-based connected trispectrum is rejected by both mock suites, with fitted amplitudes $\lesssim0.1$; (iv) fibre assignment does not change the covariance beyond what the model predicts. We present the validation methodology in detail---noise-calibrated residual maps, whitened eigenvalue spectra against null ensembles, cross-validated principal directions, template likelihoods, and bin-width variations that separate Gaussian-like from non-Gaussian errors---and discuss which terms these mocks can and cannot validate. From the redshift distributions of all mocks we find that twelve of them have a defective $n(z)$ (excluded here), that the others contain $\simeq60$\% of the linear-theory variance in the radial modes on the scale of the redshift shell, which dominate super-sample covariance, and that the two Galactic caps share these modes.}

\keywords{galaxy clustering, power spectrum, covariance matrix, redshift surveys}
